🏦 Banking77 Intent Routing Engine

Bridging the gap between baseline ML and production-grade LLM/RAG systems through rigorous evaluation.

🎯 The "Why"

Before scaling to expensive LLMs, you must understand the structural ceiling of simpler models. This project builds a two-tier intent router to prove that contextual embeddings combined with business-logic guardrails can outperform naive prompting for specific routing tasks, while keeping inference cheap and explainable.

🛠️ The Two-Tier Architecture

  • Layer 1 (TF-IDF + LogReg): Established the lexical floor at 83.8% Macro-F1. Exposed the "anchor word" limitations where models rely on single keywords instead of context.
  • Layer 2 (DistilBERT + PyTorch): Fine-tuned contextual embeddings to push accuracy to 92.6%. Overcame TF-IDF's structural ceiling by understanding word order and semantic nuance.
  • Data Pipeline Rigor: Enforced strict label_map consistency across train/test splits to prevent silent index-mismatch bugs during offline evaluation.

💡 Key Diagnostic Insights (Beyond Accuracy)

1. Aggressors vs. Victims (FP vs FN)

A high False Positive (Aggressor) intent steals queries from others. A high False Negative (Victim) intent is being robbed. Fixing the Aggressor's confidence threshold automatically heals the Victim's recall without touching its training data.

2. Symmetric Leakage = Label Ambiguity

When Intent A and Intent B leak into each other equally (e.g., Precision = Recall = 0.825), it's not a weak model. It's an inconsistent answer key. The fix is merging intents or tightening annotation guidelines, not upgrading the architecture.

3. Evaluation is a Steering Wheel

Loss curves only tell you if the model is converging. Confusion matrices and gross error counts (FN + FP) tell you where the business risk lies and whether to apply a threshold, merge labels, or route to a human agent.
